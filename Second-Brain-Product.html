Second Brain

An AI study coach that works from your own material, in your language, inside the chat app you already use.

What it is

Second Brain is an AI study coach that runs inside the chat app you already use. You send it everything you're learning from: YouTube lectures, PDFs, articles and voice notes. Then you tell it your goal. It builds a plan from your own material, quizzes you every day, shows what you haven't covered yet, and answers questions in your language. It also works across your whole class.

In one line: Tell it your goal. It turns everything you've saved into a plan, quizzes you, shows what you're missing, and works with your whole batch, in your language.

Who it's for

Primary user: an Indian college student with a deadline coming up: end-semester exams, a placement round, a technical interview or GATE.

What their situation looks like today:

  • Their material is scattered: 15 YouTube lectures, a professor's PDF, a senior's handwritten notes, forwards in the batch group, and voice notes to themselves.
  • They study on their phone, often late at night, in a mix of English and their home language.
  • They study with friends and share resources in class groups.
  • They don't want to organise anything. They want to be ready by the exam date.

Features

1. Dump: save anything in one step

  • Forward a YouTube link, PDF, article link or voice note to the bot. That's all you do.
  • No folders, tags or titles. The bot replies with a short confirmation:

✅ Saved: "Normalization in DBMS – Gate Smashers" (18 min). Covers 1NF, 2NF, 3NF, BCNF. Linked to your DBMS goal.

  • Voice notes in any Indian language are transcribed and saved like any other source. "Sir said Unit 4 is important, focus on deadlocks" becomes a searchable note.
  • Bulk save: send a whole YouTube playlist or several PDFs at once.

2. Connect: organisation happens on its own

  • Every source is read, transcribed and split into small pieces, each tied to its exact place: a video timestamp or a PDF page.
  • Each piece is matched to the topics in your goals automatically.
  • Related material is shown only when it's useful, while you're studying a topic or asking a question. There's no cluttered graph to maintain.

3. Goal Mode: start from a deadline ⭐ (Differentiator 1)

You set a goal and Second Brain plans everything toward it.

You: I have my DBMS exam on Nov 5
Second Brain: 13 days to go. Which syllabus? [VTU DBMS] [AKTU DBMS] [Paste your own]

What you get:

  • A day-by-day plan, built only from your saved material:

Day 4 · Normalization (~40 min)
▶️ Gate Smashers video, 03:10–15:40
📄 Prof's PDF, pages 14–19
📝 5-question quiz

  • The plan adjusts as you go. Missing a day, or doing badly on a quiz, moves things around automatically. Weak topics get more time; strong ones get less.
  • A daily nudge at a time you choose: "Day 4 is ready. Normalization, 40 min. Start?"
  • A countdown view: days left, topics done, topics at risk.
  • Several goals at once, such as DBMS and OS exams in the same week, with time split between them.

4. Gap Detection: what you're missing ⭐ (Differentiator 2)

Second Brain compares your saved material with what the goal requires and shows the difference.

📊 DBMS coverage: 3 of 5 units
✅ ER Model: 4 sources
✅ SQL: 3 sources
✅ Normalization: 2 sources
⚠️ Indexing: 1 weak source
❌ Transactions & Concurrency: nothing saved
→ Want 2 well-rated lectures for Transactions? [Yes] [I'll find my own]

  • What it can compare against: a university syllabus, a pasted job description ("Here's the Amazon SDE JD, what am I missing?"), or a standard list like GATE CSE topics.
  • Two separate measures:
    • Coverage gaps: you haven't saved anything on the topic.
    • Mastery gaps: you saved material but keep getting the quiz questions wrong.
  • Gaps show up early, on day 1 rather than the night before the exam.

5. Batch Brain: one brain for the class ⭐ (Differentiator 3)

Add Second Brain to your class group. Anything anyone shares there becomes part of a shared batch brain.

  • Shared material: lecture links, the professor's PDFs, a topper's notes and a senior's question bank all go in automatically.
  • Personal progress: your goals, plan, quizzes and mastery stay your own.
  • Credit for sources:

Normalization removes redundancy by… (from Riya's notes, p.22 · your voice note, Oct 12)

  • Batch insights (anonymous): "Most of the class is weak on Transactions. Riya's notes on it are rated the most helpful."
  • Joining: someone adds the bot to the group, or you join with a class code such as CSE-B-2027.
  • Privacy control: each source can be marked private or share with batch. Voice notes are private by default.

6. Ask: answers from your own material, in your language

  • Ask by text or voice, in English, Hindi, Tamil, Hinglish or a mix:

🎙️ "Kal exam hai, 2NF aur 3NF ka difference simple mein samjha do"

  • Answers come from your saved material first, in the same language you asked in.
  • Every answer links to its source: the exact video moment or PDF page. Tap it to go straight there.
  • It's honest when it doesn't know: "Nothing you've saved covers this. Here's a general explanation, and it's now marked as a gap."
  • Questions about your goal work too: "What should I revise tonight?", "Which topics am I weakest in?", "Make me a one-page cheat sheet for Unit 3."

7. Quiz and mastery tracking

  • Daily quizzes of 5 questions, mixing multiple-choice, short answer and "explain it in one line."
  • Questions come only from your sources, and each one links to where the answer is.
  • Every answer updates mastery for that topic, and the plan adjusts.
  • Exam-eve mode: a quick revision quiz on your weakest topics, plus a one-page summary of each.

8. Do: turn your knowledge into something to hand in

Beyond studying, you can ask for outputs built from what you've saved:

  • A revision cheat sheet or a one-page summary of a unit
  • Likely exam questions with model answers, from your material
  • Interview prep: "Make 10 questions an Amazon interviewer might ask from what I've studied"
  • Project help: "Help me improve image alignment in my project using what I saved about SIFT and RANSAC"

Every output cites its sources, so you can check it.

9. Dashboard (web)

A simple page that works on phone or laptop:

  • Goals: a countdown, the plan calendar and progress for each goal
  • Coverage map: each topic marked green, amber or red
  • Mastery chart: how quiz scores change over time
  • Library: everything saved, searchable, grouped by goal
  • Batch view: shared sources and class-wide weak spots

A user's journey

Day 0, setup (2 minutes)
Aditi's classmate adds Second Brain to the CSE-B group. Aditi taps start and chooses Hinglish. The bot has already picked up 23 sources the class shared over the past week.

Day 0, first goal
She types "DBMS exam Nov 5" and picks the VTU syllabus. She gets a 13-day plan and a coverage report: Transactions has nothing saved. She taps Yes for suggested lectures.

Days 1–12, daily rhythm

  • 9 PM: a nudge arrives. She does 40 minutes of planned material and a 5-question quiz.
  • During the day: she forwards new videos and voice notes. They're saved and placed in her plan without any effort from her.
  • Late-night doubt: she asks in Hinglish by voice and gets an answer linked to the exact minute of her professor's lecture.
  • Day 6: she misses a day, and the plan quietly rebalances.

Day 12, exam eve
Exam-eve mode gives her a quiz on her three weakest topics and a one-page cheat sheet for each unit.

After the exam
She sets her next goal ("OS exam Nov 12") or a placement goal. Everything she's saved carries over and counts toward the new goal.

How the experience should feel

  • Low effort. If something asks the user to organise, it's a failed design.
  • Goal first. Every screen answers "Am I on track?"
  • Grounded. Every answer and question can be traced to the user's own source.
  • Your language. The user picks the language. Mixing languages is normal, not an edge case.
  • Social, but private. The class shares material, while each person's progress stays their own.
  • Calm. One nudge a day, no streak guilt, and no feed to scroll.

What it isn't

  • Not a note-taking app. You never write formatted notes in it.
  • Not a general chatbot. It answers from your material first and marks anything else as general knowledge.
  • Not a course platform. It doesn't sell content. It uses what you already have.
  • Not a knowledge graph to manage. Connections stay out of sight until they help.
